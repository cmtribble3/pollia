Pollia polarized surface emitter

Mitsuba plugin: pollia_polarized (src/emitters/pollia_polarized.cpp).
Attach it to a shape like Mitsuba's area emitter. All area sampling, texture
sampling, visibility, two-sided emission and radiance units follow area.cpp.
The normal-based source convention follows PolRAY's planar AreaLightObject;
it extends that convention to other surface shapes using their sampled normal.
PolRAY's separate sphere-light convention is not claimed to be reproduced.

Source parameters
  radiance: emitted S0, in power per area per steradian, using a spectrum/texture.
  dop: degree of polarization [0,1], default 0.
  aolp: orientation in degrees [-90,90], default 0.
  ellipticity: signed minor/major ratio [-1,1], default 0.
  twosided, sample_texture: inherited area-emitter sampling behavior.

Let psi = radians(aolp), chi = atan(ellipticity), p = dop. The normalized state is
  [1, p cos(2 psi) cos(2 chi), p sin(2 psi) cos(2 chi), p sin(2 chi)].
Multiplying by radiance preserves S0; these controls do not simulate an optical
polarizer or introduce an extra throughput loss. Positive ellipticity gives
positive V (Mitsuba's right circular convention). Source parameters are finite
and bounded at construction. Radiance may vary by channel, while the normalized
polarization state is shared across channels. Independent per-channel Stokes
input, as supported by PolRAY's PolarizedLightSpectrum, is not yet exposed.
For a uniform one-sided source, integrated radiant flux is pi * area * radiance;
the plugin accepts radiance rather than PolRAY's radiantFluxWatts field.

Reference frame
  PolRAY reference: Packages/PolRayRenderers/Sources/PolRayRenderers/MetalShared/
  LightSampling.h, lightPSGReferenceBasis/projectPSGBasisToSegment.
  Project world-up onto the surface plane; derive x from cross(y, normal).
  Near the world-up pole, project world-right instead. Project that reference x
  into each propagation plane, falling back to the projected y at degeneracy.
  Rotate the Stokes state into Mitsuba's implicit world basis for the physical
  direction of light travel: -ds.d for next-event samples, si.to_world(si.wi)
  for emitter hits, and ray.d for emitted rays.
  This intentionally uses world axes, matching the planar PolRAY source.

Transport
  Mueller applies its accumulated matrix to the complete source vector.
  PIS applies the accumulated Jones product before a sampled depolarizer, and
  uses the camera Stokes column times the source-adjoint dot product afterward.
  Next-event evaluation mixes Jones-transformed emission and ideal-depolarized
  emission. Both emitter-hit and direct-light paths retain Q/U/V, with no
  normalization by S0 (zero-radiance sources remain well-defined).

Cornell and GUI
  rendering/pollia/scenes.py derives the custom Cornell from mi.cornell_box(),
  preserving all eight shape transforms, primitive types and reflectance colors.
  Every surface uses pollia_jarecki and the light uses pollia_polarized.
  GUI: Cornell only, Mueller (default) or PIS. RGB polarized is the default;
  monochrome remains selectable. Both custom integrators transport all RGB
  channels in one render, retaining independent channel throughput and output.
  The current Jarecki model uses achromatic Jones parameters and mixture
  probability, so its Mueller/Jones payload and PIS event are shared across
  colors. Spectral reflectance and source radiance are evaluated per channel.
  Roulette uses the maximum channel throughput; a path stops when all channels
  are dark. This adds no wavelength dispersion to the material model.
  Source controls are serialized through the worker and included in channel
  export metadata. Fresh Estimate applies changes and resets accumulation.
  Other Python presets and stock Path remain available for research/validation.

Verification
  rendering/tests/polarized_check.py checks source formulas and reference-frame
  geometry independently with NumPy, emission eval/sample consistency, both V
  signs, U orientation, partial/zero polarization, invalid controls, unchanged
  Cornell geometry, direct-light agreement with Mitsuba Path, pure-depolarizer
  equality, statistically paired multi-bounce PIS/Mueller estimates, isolated
  R/G/B source renders without channel leakage, early RGB roulette, dark
  sources, and RGB/mono Metal execution. test_polarized.py runs fresh processes
  for the two variants. test_worker.py checks both custom Cornell integrators
  through the actual shared-memory protocol.
  These numerical checks are not experimental validation of material parameters.

RGB transport verification
  Direct-light PIS/Mueller comparisons use a 2e-4 error bound relative to the
  corresponding S0 plus 2e-6 absolute error, allowing Q/U/V cancellation near
  zero. Observed maximum RGB error/S0 was 1.1382e-4; the Mueller/stock Path
  comparison had maximum error/S0 of 2.3069e-7. These are numerical tolerances,
  not experimental validation. RGB full films contain 15 channels: R/G/B,
  followed by four RGB Stokes groups. The existing display supports combined
  RGB intensity/analyzer images and individual color polarization filters.

Shared math organization
  General Jones/Mueller/Stokes math and source basis projection live in
  include/mitsuba/render/pollia_polarization.h. Both integrators, the Jarecki
  material and this emitter use that library, including its shared MIS helper.
  Cosine sampling/PDFs use Mitsuba's core/warp.h directly. The shared header
  appears directly under Pollia in the source navigator.
  Mitsuba's complex Fresnel routine conjugates positive-imaginary eta. The
  shared Fresnel helper preserves the existing PolRAY convention rather than
  substituting that routine and changing material handedness.
